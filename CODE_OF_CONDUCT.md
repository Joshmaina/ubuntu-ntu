# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project exists because languages and the people who speak them have value. That
principle applies to everyone taking part.

We are committed to a harassment-free experience for all, regardless of age, body size,
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

---

## Standards specific to this project

Beyond the usual, this project works with language and culture, which makes certain
behaviours especially damaging.

### On language varieties

**No variety is broken, corrupted, or lesser.** Rural, urban, diaspora, youth, and
mixed varieties are all legitimate. Comments describing a way of speaking as "not real
Yoruba," "bad Swahili," or "corrupted by English" are not welcome here.

**Disagreement about correctness is usually variation.** When two speakers say different
things, the first assumption should be that both are right for their own variety — not
that one is wrong. Ask where someone is from before correcting them.

**Nobody arbitrates a language they do not speak.** Maintainers do not overrule native
speakers on linguistic correctness. Where speakers disagree, we record both forms with
their origins rather than declaring a winner.

### On cultural material

**Some knowledge is not ours to publish.** If a community indicates that material is
sacred, ceremonial, restricted by initiation, or otherwise not for general circulation,
that decision is final and requires no justification to us.

**Do not contribute someone else's words without their consent.** Recording an elder and
uploading it without their informed permission is a serious violation, regardless of
intent.

### On expertise

**Speaking a language is expertise.** A contributor with no formal training who has
spoken a language since childhood knows things a linguist with three degrees does not.
Treat that knowledge as authoritative.

**Formal training is also expertise.** Linguists bring analytical tools that speakers
may not have. Neither form of knowledge outranks the other.

---

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Sexualised language or imagery; unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Dismissing or mocking a language, variety, accent, or culture
- Contributing recordings of others without consent
- Sustained disruption of discussions
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

---

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces — repository, issues, discussions, pull requests — and when
representing the project publicly.

It also applies to interactions with **language communities** whether or not they use
this repository. Approaching a community disrespectfully while representing this project
is a violation.

---

## Enforcement

Report concerns to the maintainer. All reports are reviewed and investigated promptly and
fairly. The privacy and safety of the reporter is respected.

> **Current limitation, stated honestly:** enforcement currently rests with a single
> maintainer. That is not an adequate structure for handling a serious report,
> particularly one concerning the maintainer. As the project grows, enforcement moves to
> the governance group described in
> [Data Governance §11](docs/09-DATA-GOVERNANCE.md). Until then, anyone uncomfortable
> reporting to the maintainer may raise the matter publicly in a discussion, and that is
> a legitimate route rather than an escalation.

### Consequences

1. **Correction** — a private explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate
2. **Warning** — with consequences for continued behaviour
3. **Temporary ban** from project spaces
4. **Permanent ban**
